# Multiframe — Product Requirements Document

**Version:** 1.0
**Date:** 2026-09-10
**Status:** Baseline — approved for Phase 1 execution

---

## 1. Product Manifesto & Positioning

### 1.1 Mission

Multiframe is a pure computational photography engine for Android. Every pixel in
its output is optically measured. No pixel is hallucinated, inferred, inpainted,
upscaled by a neural network, or generated by a model trained on other
photographs. The app performs the same operations a darkroom or a raw processor
performs — averaging, alignment, tone mapping, sharpening — at sensor-native
resolution, at capture time, on the device.

### 1.2 Anti-AI Stance

This is the product's core differentiator and is non-negotiable:

- **No generative models.** No diffusion, no GAN, no neural super-resolution, no
  learned demosaic, no AI denoise. If a technique cannot be expressed as a
  closed-form function of the measured photon counts, it does not ship.
- **No cloud processing.** No frames leave the device. No network permission is
  requested.
- **No "computational photography" that invents detail.** The merge averages
  measured samples. The super-resolution stitches measured tiles. The tone curve
  redistributes measured light. Each operation has a mathematical proof that its
  output is a function of its input and nothing else.

The user promise: "If you see it in the picture, the sensor saw it in the scene."

### 1.3 Competitive Positioning

| Axis | Stock OEM (Pixel Camera, Samsung Pro) | Adobe Project Indigo | Multiframe |
|---|---|---|---|
| Noise reduction | Neural network hallucination | Neural network (optional) | Temporal averaging of measured frames |
| Dynamic range | Multi-frame HDR with tone fusion | Multi-frame HDR with local tone mapping | DCG bracketed merge + bilateral grid local tone map |
| Super-resolution | AI upscaling | AI-assisted | Optical: telephoto tile stitching (Brenizer method) |
| Output format | JPEG/HEIC, processed | DNG + JPEG | DNG (merged raw) + JPEG (developed) |
| Manual control | Limited | Full (on import) | Full Camera2 manual exposure, focus, WB at capture |
| Offline | Yes | No (cloud features) | Yes (zero network) |

### 1.4 Target Audience

Photographers who want DSLR-grade output from a phone and are willing to learn a
professional interface to get it. Not casual shooters. The app does not simplify
photography; it makes phone hardware perform at its physical limit.

## 2. Core Feature Specifications

### 2.1 Viewfinder & Tactile HUD

The viewfinder is the product. It must feel like a rangefinder's optical
viewfinder — immediate, clean, and informative without being cluttered.

#### 2.1.1 Zero-Latency Preview

- Source: `AHardwareBuffer`-backed ZSL ring buffer, zero-copy to preview surface.
- The preview surface receives only the low-ISO request in DCG mode (high-ISO
  frames excluded via `removeTarget(previewSurface)`).
- Frame rate: sensor-native (typically 30 fps; 15 fps during DCG, which is
  acceptable).
- No post-processing on the preview path. The viewfinder shows what the sensor
  sees, not what the develop will produce.

#### 2.1.2 Technical Typography

All live readings (ISO, shutter speed, EV, focus distance, histogram, tile
count, coverage percentage) use monospaced type at the accent color (`Ink.Amber`)
on a semi-transparent readout ground (`Ink.Readout`). Labels use proportional
type at the secondary color (`Ink.Muted`) with wide letter spacing. This is the
instrument convention: the number is the reading, the word is the label, and the
two are visually distinct.

Formalized type scale:

| Token | Size | Weight | Family | Color | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Gauge` | 13sp | Normal | Monospace | `Amber` | Live readings: ISO, shutter, EV, coverage %, zoom |
| `GaugeLarge` | 26sp | Normal | Monospace | `Amber` | Prominent readings: tile count during sweep |
| `Label` | 11sp | Medium | Default | `Muted` | Static labels next to readings: "ISO", "SHUTTER", "EV" |
| `Control` | 12sp | Medium | Default | `Bone` | Chip/button text: "MERGE ON", "FRAMES", "GUIDES" |
| `Section` | 10sp | Medium | Default | `Muted` | Panel section headers, uppercase, 1.2sp tracking |
| `Status` | 12sp | Normal | Monospace | `White` | Transient status messages: "raw burst...", "12 frames" |
| `Countdown` | 76sp | Normal | Monospace | `Bone` | Self-timer countdown numeral |
| `Instruction` | 10sp | Medium | Default | `Muted` | Guidance text: "PAN SLOWLY", uppercase, 1.2sp tracking |

#### 2.1.3 Optical Status Gauges

- **Exposure readout:** ISO, shutter speed, EV compensation, MAN indicator.
  Rendered as a single horizontal bar with dot separators. Background:
  `Ink.Readout` rounded rect.
- **Histogram:** 132x40dp, same visual treatment as exposure readout. Adjacent
  to the readout, not in a separate corner.
- **Focus score:** Shown only during manual focus. Numeric sharpness metric in
  `Ink.Amber` on `Ink.Readout`.
- **Digital zoom:** Shown only when zoom > 1.02x. Same treatment.
- **Level gauge:** Drawn directly on the viewfinder. Green (`Ink.Level`) when
  level, fades when tilted. This is a documented exception to "nothing coloured
  on the photograph."

#### 2.1.4 Mechanical-Feel Haptic Feedback

The app must provide tactile confirmation for every state change. Haptic
feedback is not decoration — it is the primary confirmation channel when the
user's eyes are on the subject, not the screen.

| Gesture | Haptic | Constant |
|---|---|---|
| Shutter press (capture initiated) | Strong confirmation | `HapticFeedbackConstants.CONFIRM` |
| Self-timer tick (each second) | Light tick | `HapticFeedbackConstants.CLOCK_TICK` |
| Mode/toggle change (MERGE, DCG, ZSL) | Segment tick | `HapticFeedbackConstants.SEGMENT_FREQUENT_TICK` |
| Slider value at detent (ISO round number, EV zero) | Tick | `HapticFeedbackConstants.CLOCK_TICK` |
| Sweep tile accepted | Confirmation | `HapticFeedbackConstants.CONFIRM` |
| Sweep tile rejected | Rejection | `HapticFeedbackConstants.REJECT` |
| Error / invalid action | Rejection | `HapticFeedbackConstants.REJECT` |
| Lens switch | Segment tick | `HapticFeedbackConstants.SEGMENT_FREQUENT_TICK` |

Implementation: `View.performHapticFeedback()` via `LocalView.current` in
Compose, called from the control dispatch (`handleControl` lambda in
`CameraScreen`).

#### 2.1.5 Progressive Disclosure

Controls are layered by frequency of use:

1. **Always visible:** Shutter button, lens strip, exposure readout, histogram.
2. **Top row (scrollable):** MERGE, FRAMES, MODE, ZSL, TIMER, GUIDES, PRO,
   INFO. Six visible at once on a standard phone.
3. **PRO panel (toggle):** Manual exposure (ISO, shutter, EV), manual focus, WB
   presets, A/B mode, highlight guard, DCG, ISP bypass, reset.
4. **Action strip (beside shutter):** SUPER RES, RAW burst, DNG. These write
   files and are separated from settings by shape (squared, not pilled).

The PRO panel is "what you set once." The top row is "what you change between
shots." The distinction is enforced by the `ControlBar` / `ControlKind` type
system.

### 2.2 Dual Conversion Gain (DCG) & Exposure Engine

#### 2.2.1 What DCG Is

Software ISO bracketing via `CameraCaptureSession.setRepeatingBurst()`. The ZSL
ring alternates between low-ISO and high-ISO capture requests. Low-ISO frames
preserve highlights; high-ISO frames recover shadows. The merge normalizes by
`gainScale = frameIso / refIso` and relies on robustness weighting to reject
clipped high-ISO highlights.

This is **not** hardware dual conversion gain (a sensor-level analog circuit).
The app simulates the benefit of DCG through exposure bracketing, which works on
any sensor with manual ISO control and RAW output.

#### 2.2.2 Automated Noise-Variance Threshold Detection

**Goal:** Determine whether the sensor has a hardware DCG threshold (a
discontinuity in the read-noise-vs-ISO curve) and, if so, bracket at that
boundary for maximum benefit.

**Method:**

1. At first launch (or when a new physical camera is detected), capture a
   sequence of short-exposure frames at ISO values stepping from `isoMin` to
   `isoMax` in 20 steps.
2. Compute temporal variance per frame (from the black-level-subtracted raw
   values in a central crop to avoid vignette).
3. Fit two linear segments to the variance-vs-ISO curve. If the breakpoint
   improves the fit significantly (F-test, p < 0.01), record that ISO as the DCG
   threshold.
4. Store the result in `SharedPreferences` keyed by physical camera ID.
5. If a threshold is detected, set `DCG_RATIO = thresholdIso / isoMin` (clamped
   to [2, 8]).

**Constraint:** This must be non-invasive. No lens cap, no dark room, no user
involvement. The frames are captured with the shortest possible exposure during
normal preview startup, using the existing ZSL ring. The user never sees them.

#### 2.2.3 ZSL Ring Architecture

- `RingBuffer` (C++): mmap-backed `AHardwareBuffer` ring. Frames are written by
  the camera HAL and read by the merge without copying.
- Ring size: dynamic, computed by `MemoryBudget` from the device heap and frame
  resolution.
- `setRepeatingBurst(listOf(lowRequest, highRequest))` when DCG enabled.
- `setRepeatingRequest(singleRequest)` when DCG disabled (identical to baseline).
- Each request tagged via `CaptureRequest.Builder.setTag("low" / "high")`.
- High-ISO request: `AE_MODE=OFF`, ISO = converged_low * `DCG_RATIO`, same
  exposure time as the low-ISO AE convergence.
- AE re-convergence: rebuild the high-ISO request when AE converges to a new ISO
  (threshold: >10% change).

#### 2.2.4 Highlight-Protecting Merge

- Reference frame: the lowest-ISO frame in the burst.
- Each frame's `gainScale` computed from `SENSOR_SENSITIVITY` metadata:
  `frameIso.toFloat() / refIso.toFloat()`.
- In the merge inner loop: `altV = rawAlt * invGain` (where `invGain = 1 /
  gainScale`) before difference computation and accumulation.
- Robustness weighting: a high-ISO frame that has clipped (value near
  `whiteLevel`) produces a large normalized difference from the reference and
  receives weight near zero. No separate clipping detection is needed.
- The `gainScale = 1.0f` default is a mechanical no-op: every existing code path
  produces identical output. The A/A null test is the proof.

#### 2.2.5 Exposure Strategy

- `ExposureStrategy.shadowRecoveryStops(frameCount)`: sqrt(N) improvement from
  temporal averaging.
- `ExposureStrategy.shadowRecoveryStopsDcg(frameCount, dcgRatio)`: base +
  `0.5 * log2(dcgRatio)` additional stops from the high-ISO frames.
- `protectHighlights()`: pulls exposure down by up to the shadow recovery budget,
  preserving highlights that would otherwise clip. The pull amount is the
  `guardPull` shown in the HUD.

### 2.3 Guided Telephoto Super-Resolution (The Brenizer Engine)

#### 2.3.1 Concept

Use the telephoto lens (longer focal length, higher per-pixel resolution) to
capture a grid of tiles that, stitched together, cover the wide-angle lens's
field of view at the telephoto's native resolution. This is the Brenizer method
(panoramic bokeh) applied to resolution rather than depth of field.

Example: a 110mm telephoto covering a 24mm wide-angle framing produces a ~4.6x
linear resolution gain. At 12 MP per tile and 49 tiles (7x7), the theoretical
output is 263 MP. Practically, overlap reduces this; the default cap is 80 MP /
610 MB working memory.

#### 2.3.2 User-Selectable Matrices

Three presets replace the fixed 7x7 grid:

| Preset | Grid | Tiles | Use Case | Approx. Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Detail Boost** | 2x2 | 4 | Quick detail enhancement, handheld-friendly | ~20 MP |
| **Large Format** | 3x3 | 9 | Medium-format equivalent, manageable sweep | ~45 MP |
| **Custom Bounding Box** | User-drawn | Variable | Full control over coverage area | Variable |

The user selects the preset before starting the sweep. The viewfinder shows the
resulting grid overlaid on the wide-angle framing.

Custom Bounding Box: the user draws a rectangle on the wide-angle preview. The
planner computes the minimum tile grid that covers the rectangle at the
telephoto's resolution, with the required overlap.

#### 2.3.3 Reticle/Dot Guidance System

**This replaces the free-roam panning arrow concept.**

When the sweep begins:

1. The viewfinder switches to the telephoto lens.
2. Target dots appear overlaid on the viewfinder, one per tile position. Each dot
   represents the center of a tile the user needs to capture.
3. A crosshair reticle is fixed at the center of the viewfinder.
4. The user pans the phone to align the reticle with each target dot.
5. When the reticle is within the lock radius of a target dot (configurable,
   default 5% of frame width):
   - The dot changes color (muted -> amber -> green on lock).
   - A haptic confirmation fires (`CONFIRM`).
   - The system automatically captures a burst at that position.
   - The dot fills solid green to indicate completion.
6. If the user moves too quickly (alignment residual exceeds threshold), the dot
   flashes red and fires `REJECT` haptic. The user must re-align.

**Advantages over free-roam panning:**

- Deterministic tile placement (no gaps, no redundant captures).
- Natural stopping points reduce hand fatigue.
- Haptic confirmation means the user doesn't need to watch the screen.
- Works for 4 tiles (Detail Boost) without feeling heavy.

#### 2.3.4 Capture and Assembly Pipeline

For each locked tile:

1. Capture a burst (frame count from current setting, typically 4-8).
2. Merge the burst (same pipeline as normal capture, with `gainScale` if DCG is
   active).
3. Record the tile's homography relative to the first tile (estimated from
   feature matching between overlapping regions).
4. Composite onto the mmap-backed canvas with feathered blending.

**Post-sweep assembly:**

1. Bundle adjustment: refine all tile homographies simultaneously to minimize
   global reprojection error. This is a Levenberg-Marquardt optimization on the
   8 parameters per homography. No external solver library — the Jacobian is
   sparse and the problem is small (max ~50 tiles x 8 params).
2. Apply lens distortion correction (from `LensProfile`) to each tile before
   final compositing.
3. Multi-band Laplacian blending (3-4 pyramid levels) to handle exposure and
   color temperature differences between tiles.
4. Output: single merged raw (DNG) or developed JPEG at the canvas resolution.

#### 2.3.5 Memory Management

- Canvas allocation via `MosaicCanvas` backed by mmap.
- Working memory: one tile (12 MP x 8 bytes = 96 MB) + canvas (variable).
- 80 MP cap = 610 MB canvas. The `MemoryBudget` enforces this against the device
  heap.
- Tiles are composited as they arrive — only one tile is held in memory at a
  time alongside the canvas.

### 2.4 Integrated Review & Gallery

#### 2.4.1 Instant Post-Capture Review

After every capture, the developed JPEG appears in a review overlay:

- Full-screen display with pinch-to-zoom (up to 100% pixel-peep at native
  resolution).
- Merge readout: "12 frames · 89% kept · DCG" overlaid at the bottom.
- Swipe up to dismiss and return to viewfinder. Swipe down or tap to save.
- For sweep captures: the stitched result with a coverage map toggle.

#### 2.4.2 Gallery Screen

A dedicated screen accessible from the last-shot thumbnail in the viewfinder.

- Grid layout: 3-column on phones, 4-column on tablets.
- Grouped by capture session (date + time cluster).
- Each entry shows one thumbnail regardless of whether DNG + JPEG were saved.
  Badge indicates DNG presence.
- Tap to open full-screen review with pinch-to-zoom.
- Long-press for EXIF overlay: focal length, ISO, shutter, aperture, merge
  stats, DCG status, file size.
- Actions: Share (via `Intent.ACTION_SEND`), Delete (with confirmation).

#### 2.4.3 DNG/JPEG Management

- Paired files (same base name, different extension) are treated as a single
  capture.
- Deleting a capture deletes both files.
- Sharing defaults to JPEG. A "Share DNG" option is available in the detail view.
- Storage location: `MediaStore.Images` via `ContentResolver`. No direct file
  access.

## 3. Non-Functional Requirements & Performance Budgets

### 3.1 Memory

| Resource | Budget | Enforcement |
|---|---|---|
| ZSL ring buffer | Computed by `MemoryBudget` from heap and resolution | Dynamic frame count |
| Merge working set | 20 bytes/pixel (two float accumulators + result + ARGB + bitmap) | Fixed by pipeline |
| Mosaic canvas | Max 610 MB (80 MP default cap) | `MosaicPlanner.canvasBytes` check |
| Total app heap | Must not exceed 75% of `Runtime.maxMemory()` | `MemoryPressure` monitor, trim on `onTrimMemory` |

### 3.2 Latency

| Operation | Budget | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Shutter press to capture start | < 5ms | ZSL: burst is already in the ring |
| Burst capture (8 frames at 30fps) | ~267ms | Sensor-limited |
| Merge (8 frames, 12 MP, native) | < 150ms | NEON-vectorized, measured by `MergeSpeedDeviceTest` |
| Develop (12 MP, CPU) | < 300ms | Thread-pooled, measured by profiling |
| Develop (12 MP, GPU — Phase 3) | < 50ms | Target, not yet measured |
| JPEG encode (12 MP, libjpeg-turbo) | < 100ms | Striped parallel encode, measured by `JpegEncodeSpeedDeviceTest` |
| Total capture-to-review | < 800ms (CPU) / < 400ms (GPU) | Sum of above |

### 3.3 Frame Drops

- Viewfinder: zero dropped frames during normal operation (30 fps).
- During DCG: 15 fps is acceptable (half the frames go to RAW only).
- During sweep: viewfinder may drop to 15 fps during tile compositing.
- UI thread: no operation > 16ms on the main thread. All pipeline work is
  dispatched to `Dispatchers.Default`.

### 3.4 Device Compatibility

#### Primary Targets (must pass all tests, must look correct)

- **Pixel 11 Pro** (Tensor G6, 2026): Primary development device.
- **Pixel 9 Pro XL** (Tensor G4, 2024): Secondary, validates backward compat.

#### Compatibility Tier (must not crash, features degrade gracefully)

- Samsung Galaxy S25 series (Exynos 2500 / Snapdragon 8 Elite)
- OnePlus 13 (Snapdragon 8 Elite)
- Any device with: minSdk 33, Camera2 FULL or LEVEL_3, at least one RAW-capable
  rear camera.

#### Graceful Degradation Rules

- No RAW capability: app functions as a JPEG-only camera. Merge, DCG, DNG, and
  super-res are hidden.
- No manual sensor control: AE/AF remain in AUTO. Manual exposure controls are
  hidden.
- Single rear camera: lens strip is hidden, super-res is unavailable.
- No `setRepeatingBurst` support: DCG toggle is hidden, app uses
  `setRepeatingRequest` only.
- Low heap (< 256 MB): frame count capped, mosaic canvas capped at 20 MP.

## Appendix A: What Is Not In Scope

- **Video capture.** The ZSL ring is frame-oriented. Video requires a separate
  `MediaRecorder` session. This is a v2 feature.
- **Cloud sync.** Conflicts with the offline-only positioning.
- **AI denoise.** By design. If computational denoise is added in a future
  version, it must be wavelet or BM3D-family, not neural.
- **RAW editor / adjustments.** The app captures and develops. Editing is the
  domain of Lightroom, Snapseed, and darktable.
- **Social features.** No sharing to specific platforms, no filters, no stickers.

## Appendix B: Glossary

- **DCG (Dual Conversion Gain):** In this app, software ISO bracketing that
  simulates the benefit of hardware dual-conversion-gain sensors.
- **ZSL (Zero Shutter Lag):** Frames are continuously captured into a ring buffer
  before the user presses the shutter. The shutter selects frames from the ring
  rather than initiating capture.
- **Brenizer Method:** A photographic technique of stitching multiple telephoto
  shots to simulate a large-format camera's field of view at the telephoto's
  resolution and depth of field.
- **Robustness weighting:** Per-pixel weights in the merge that reduce the
  contribution of frames that disagree with the reference (due to motion, noise,
  or clipping).
- **`gainScale`:** Ratio of a frame's ISO to the reference frame's ISO. Used to
  normalize high-ISO frames to the reference exposure before merging.
- **Bilateral grid:** A 3D data structure (x, y, luminance) used for
  edge-preserving local tone mapping. Compresses dynamic range without halos.
